# Digital estate — access, preservation, transfer, and closure

Research date: October 29, 2026. The founder explicitly identified web/social-media account access and transfers as critical. Current platform procedures are evidence; successful recovery or inheritance is not assumed.

## Distinct outcomes

| Outcome | Meaning to establish for an account |
| --- | --- |
| Information/data access | Obtain a permitted copy of content. This need not grant login or control. |
| Legacy management | Exercise a platform's limited designated-contact powers. |
| Business administration | Continue an organization through an existing or approved admin role. |
| Eligible asset transfer | Use a provider-recognized ownership/account process for an asset such as a domain. |
| Memorialization | Preserve a public profile under the platform's memorial rules, potentially locking access. |
| Closure/deletion | End the account or remove content, sometimes irreversibly. |
| Subscription/billing resolution | Stop or move ongoing charges; this can be separate from profile closure. |

## Dependencies that need investigation before account changes

1. **Identify the owner's intended outcome.** Keeping family photos, maintaining a business, memorializing a profile, and deleting private material are different objectives.
2. **Identify who the provider recognizes.** An in-app contact, named beneficiary, legacy contact, business admin, and court-appointed estate representative are not interchangeable.
3. **Locate advance arrangements.** Apple keys, accepted legacy invitations, password-manager emergency arrangements, recovery materials, and business-admin roles may depend on preparation before death.
4. **Map authentication and service relationships.** Email, phone numbers, devices, password managers, SSO, domains, billing, and hosting can support other accounts. Their specific relationships must be established, not assumed.
5. **Resolve preservation before irreversible actions.** Google expressly documents loss of later content-request options after closure. LinkedIn documents locking and connection termination after memorialization.
6. **Resolve each business component separately.** A domain, website hosting, content, business Page, email service, subscription, and revenue account can have different providers and procedures.
7. **Record the actual outcome.** Requested, verified, granted, exported, transferred, memorialized, closed, and declined are different states.

Steps 1–7 are an analytical dependency model grounded in the provider differences above. They are not a universal authorization to sign in as the deceased or a validated product workflow.

## Information to test for usefulness

The following are candidate research fields, not a decided data schema:

- Provider, account type, handle/profile URL, and associated individual or organization.
- Owner's wishes for preservation, privacy, continuation, or closure.
- Whether an actual provider-level legacy/delegate/admin arrangement exists and was accepted.
- Location of necessary access keys or recovery instructions, with separate consideration of sensitive secrets.
- Recipient identity, role, and relevant authority documents.
- Connected email, phone, SSO, domain, hosting, subscription, and billing dependencies.
- Relevant provider procedure, region/account applicability, verification date, and unresolved evidence.
- Case reference, submission/response history, approved outcome, and remaining tasks.

## Remaining gaps

Current Instagram and TikTok policies need accessible primary verification. Facebook's detailed current rules need verification beyond historical announcements. Other providers, creator revenue, digital wallets, crypto-assets, gaming/media licenses, cloud/work accounts, and websites outside GoDaddy have not been established comprehensively. Authority and disclosure law also depend on jurisdiction.

Actual family experiences with these procedures have not yet been observed for this product. Public instructions establish available routes and constraints, not completion rates or usability.
